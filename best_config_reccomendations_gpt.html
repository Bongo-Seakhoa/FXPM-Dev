Bottom Line

The current profile is largely coherent and better integrated than the pre-v2 stack. I would keep the active-recent workflow, real-cost discovery, strict artifact drift blocking, institutional supervisor, cycle drift monitor, walk-forward promotion gating, swap model, and governance tournament exactly as the backbone.

The main settings I would review before funded live are not discovery settings. They are operational settings: generate the missing regime_params.json, lower advisory loss thresholds so warnings arrive before hard blocks, set a storage entry-pause floor, and only graduate shadow-mode overlays after paper evidence proves they add value.

Best immediate posture Paper or first-funded live with risk controls strict, governance/risk overlays in shadow, and live readiness fail-closed.
Best growth posture Raise trade risk only after at least one full retrain cycle plus a clean paper/live observation window.
Most important blocker A missing regime_params.json or an active supervisor latch should stop funded live until resolved.
Configuration principle Optimize edge with realistic costs; scale risk only after live behavior validates the artifact assumptions.

Complementary Feature Fit

The settings now form a useful chain: discovery uses realistic spread, commission, slippage, and swap assumptions; validation and walk-forward audits classify fragility; live readiness refuses stale or blocked artifacts; promotion status controls whether winners are skipped, paper-only, derated, or fully tradable; supervisor and margin rules contain account-level damage; cycle drift watches whether live execution quality is decaying; governance experiments with exits in shadow before it changes real trade handling.

The one friction point I still see is operational, not architectural: advisory loss thresholds are set too high to be meaningful next to the hard block thresholds. Lowering those makes the warning layer complement the supervisor instead of arriving after the supervisor has already done the important work.

Signoff View

I sign off on the current configuration architecture as internally complementary: the features now mostly reinforce one another instead of competing for control. The best immediate working state is close to the current config.json, with the operational adjustments called out above.

I do not sign off on funded live until runtime state is clean: no active supervisor latch, fresh eligible winner ledger, broker-verified specs, and the missing regime params file resolved. After that, paper-first or low-risk first-live is the right way to let the improved stack prove itself.
