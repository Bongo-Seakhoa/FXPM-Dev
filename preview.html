1 · The correction: this is not just a radar chart with a final score

Wrong / watered-down version

“Take a final spider chart and reward the biggest balanced polygon.”

This is useful, but it is incomplete. A final radar face can hide how the candidate got there. A specialist can look good at the end because of a late profit spike, a lucky cluster, or one narrow slice of evidence doing all the work.

  • It over-focuses on the final face.
  • It can miss path deformities.
  • It treats evidence as a scoreboard instead of a structural dimension.
Correct conceptual version

“Build a 3D quality object as evidence accumulates.”

The middle of the spider has a downward evidence spine. That spine is trade count n, time t, or a normalized evidence-progress index. Every side metric is a function over that spine.

  • profit_factor(n)
  • tail_CVaR(n)
  • capital_preservation(n) (reverse path-dependent drawdown; higher is better)
  • generalization_survival(n) (reverse train→OOS gap; higher is better)
  • temporal_consistency(n)
  • parameter_stability(n)

The main purpose is not speed

Efficiency is a byproduct. The main goal is better winner selection: selecting candidates whose profitability, capital preservation, robustness, and live-market reliability are supported throughout the evidence path, not merely by a good final scalar score.

Specialist-first hierarchy

The primary optimisation unit is the specialist cell: symbol × timeframe × regime. The scorer should ask which candidate is the best live-capable specialist inside that cell. Portfolio balance, symbol balance, regime balance, correlation, alpha/beta, and crowding are account-level diagnostics or future research layers; they should not suppress a specialist merely for being specialized. The portfolio layer is a survival governor first: margin safety, account drawdown, exposure limits, broker close-out prevention, and emergency kill conditions.

2 · The 3D object: central evidence spine + metric surfaces

This figure is intentionally conceptual. The system does not need to build a literal 3D mesh. The machine representation can be a tensor: quality_tensor[candidate, evidence_step, metric]. The visual language helps humans and agents understand what the tensor means.

Each radial metric can be plotted against the central evidence axis. The spider object is formed by wrapping these metric-over-evidence curves around the same spine.

The central point: the path is scored. A candidate that only becomes good at the end is not equivalent to one that builds reliable quality throughout the evidence path.

4 · Same final face, different path: why the 3D view matters

Candidate A: evidence-coherent winner

A strong candidate can have noise, but the shape expands with balance: profit, risk, stability, generalization, and distribution improve together.

Candidate B: late-rescued / misshapen

This candidate may end with good return/PF, but one or more sides remain dented. The system should not crown it unless it proves the deformity is temporary, recovered, and not structurally meaningful.

Important: do not require a perfect shape

Trading paths are noisy. Temporary dents are allowed. The framework should penalize persistent, concentrated, unrecovered, or structurally meaningful deformities — not every wiggle.

6 · Scoring model: what should be measured

Core tensor

Machine representation

quality_tensor[candidate, evidence_step, metric]

Each metric is normalized so “higher is better.” For example, raw path-dependent drawdown is reversed into capital_preservation(n), and raw CVaR is transformed into a tail-risk-control score.

Orientation note: preserve the shape by reversing drawdown

Drawdown itself is lower-is-better, which fights the geometry. The scoring axis should therefore use capital preservation: a higher-is-better curve over evidence that rises when equity avoids deep/time-persistent drawdown and dents when capital is trapped underwater. This keeps every radial metric aligned in the same positive direction.

Final face qualityHow strong the last cross-section is after sufficient evidence.
Average path qualityHow strong the candidate was throughout the evidence path.
Shape balanceWhether one metric is carrying the whole result.
Dent severityWorst axis deformation relative to the candidate’s own path and the pool.
Dent durationHow long a metric stays damaged.
Dent recoveryWhether a dent heals or remains structurally unresolved.
Late-rescue dependenceWhether final performance is rescued by one late cluster.
Train→OOS divergenceWhether the path survives outside the fitting window.
Important distinction

Trade count is evidence resolution, not the prize

More trades are useful because they give more detail about the shape. But once evidence sufficiency is reached, trade count should not keep boosting the candidate.

if trades < hard_min: reject
coverage = min(1, sqrt(trades / target_trades))

The coverage term prevents under-observed candidates from winning. It does not turn high turnover into a dominant profitability reward.

Suggested decomposition

ComponentWhat it asksExample calculationWhy it matters

Use volume, but do not worship volume

Volume is useful only when combined with integrity checks. A large misshapen object is not automatically better. The best candidate is the one with strong final quality, strong path quality, controlled dents, low concentration, and good OOS survival.

7 · Candidate selection flow

The process is not “speed first.” The process is “quality first, with conservative pruning only when a candidate’s evidence object is already structurally incapable of matching the incumbent or entering the Pareto set.”

Recommended implementation stance

Start with the simplest clean representation: candidate × evidence_step × metric. Do not over-engineer a literal 3D mesh. The geometry is the conceptual model; the tensor and derived path features are the implementation.

Transparent spokes, not opaque clusters

The eight primary spokes are independent specialist-survival questions. Each spoke should have a clear headline curve and explicit sensor provenance. Supporting sensors may inform a spoke, but they must not become a hidden black-box average. If a raw metric gives the clearest path signal, use that raw metric; if several raw metrics are highly redundant, combine or choose among them only after evidence shows the combined spoke predicts forward survival better.

9 · Human translation: what this framework is really saying

Good winner

Evidence-coherent

The candidate becomes profitable in a way that remains supported as trades/time accumulate. Risk metrics, distribution, and OOS behavior do not break while profit improves.

Suspicious winner

Late-rescued

The final score looks good, but most of the evidence path was weak. One late cluster or one narrow evidence slice rescued the specialist.

Bad winner

Structurally deformed

The candidate has persistent dents: capital-preservation damage, CVaR damage, train/OOS collapse, cluster dependence, or unrecovered instability.

10 · Clean prompt block to give to the coding agent

I want the scoring system to use a path-dependent 3D evidence geometry model.

Do not treat the spider chart as only a final radar chart. The center of the spider has a downward evidence axis. This axis represents n trades, chronological time, OOS slice progression, or normalized evidence progress.

Each radial metric is a function over this evidence axis:

edge_survival(n)
profit_structure_quality(n)
capital_preservation(n)
tail_risk_control(n)
temporal_consistency(n)
generalization_survival(n)
cost_execution_survival(n)
parameter_stability(n)

At each evidence point, the candidate has a radar cross-section. The full scoring object is the evolution of these cross-sections through the evidence axis.

The machine representation can be:

quality_tensor[candidate, evidence_step, metric]

Do not reduce the concept to geomean alone. Geomean may describe one face, but the full model must evaluate the trajectory of the shape.

The scoring should measure:

1. final face quality,
2. average path quality,
3. shape balance,
4. worst dent depth,
5. dent duration,
6. dent recovery,
7. capital-preservation damage and tail-risk concentration,
8. late-rescue dependence,
9. evidence-slice / trade-cluster concentration,
10. train-to-OOS path divergence.

Trade count is not the prize. Trade count is evidence resolution. Use it as a sufficiency gate and saturating confidence term, not as a dominant profitability reward.

The main goal is not speed. Efficiency is only a byproduct of identifying structurally inferior candidates earlier. The main goal is better live-market winner selection.

A candidate should be promoted only if its quality is supported across the evidence path, not merely because its final scalar score is high.

Temporary dents are acceptable. Persistent, concentrated, unrecovered, or structurally meaningful deformities should penalize or reject the candidate.

Implement this in the simplest clean way inside the existing FXPM-Dev scoring architecture. Preserve cache/fingerprint safety. Make every pruning or promotion decision explainable.

Do not portfolio-balance the specialist

Do not penalize a candidate inside a symbol/timeframe/regime cell because it is specialized. Portfolio diagnostics such as symbol balance, regime balance, exposure concentration, correlation, and crowding may be measured later, but they are not primary candidate-scoring axes unless a future experiment proves they improve live outcomes without reducing edge.
