FxPM 1.4 — Comprehensive End-to-End Review

Scope: full source + config + docs review for live-trading robustness and PnL quality  |  Target: pm_core.py, pm_pipeline.py, pm_main.py, pm_strategies.py, pm_mt5.py, pm_position.py, pm_regime.py, pm_optuna.py, pm_enhancement_seams.py, pm_storage.py, config.json, documentation  |  Codebase: ~21,300 LOC across 10 modules, 47-strategy pool, 4-regime detector, Numba backtest, MT5 execution  |  Review date: 2026-04-21

Executive summary

The FxPM 1.4 codebase is structurally mature: the regime-aware selection model, the Numba backtest with same-bar gap-through-stop parity, the calendar-anchored retrain schedule, the winners-only live policy, the MT5 contract-aware sizing, and the broker/spread-quality overlay together form a disciplined, correctness-first trading stack. Backtest-to-live data parity is tight (see audit.md) and restart recovery is robust.

The PnL risk is not in execution — it is concentrated in validation gate tightness, scoring-weight design, and the committed challenge-profile config.json, which is materially looser than the in-code defaults in several quality-critical places. The review also surfaces a handful of execution edge cases (spread-median windowing, lot-normalization drift, decision-throttle persistence, dead-code in the position layer) that will bite asymmetrically in live vs. paper.

The single highest-leverage intervention is to realign the committed config with the code-default validation gates (regime PF, regime return floor, exceptional-val thresholds, robustness ratio, actionable margin) before live deployment. The second is to tighten selection-time discipline at the active fx_backtester scorer (PF emphasis, continuous DD penalty, weak-train rescue gate). The third is the strategic gap surfaced by the holistic review pass — the system today is a strong per-symbol research/execution engine but still needs clearer downstream review discipline, better cost realism, and a first-class post-entry order-governance subsystem built around exact local winners rather than one global umbrella policy. See Section 13.

1. Governing philosophy & review lens

The review is conducted strictly within the design values already encoded in README.md, SETTINGS_REFERENCE.md, PATCH_NOTES.md, and the archived audits. Those values are:

  • Winners-only, no fallback — validation failure is a no-trade, not a "best available" trade.
  • Correctness before performance — Numba speed is opt-in; the Python path is authoritative when they conflict.
  • Config is the source of truth — defensive code defaults are a safety net, not operating values.
  • Candle-close decision timing — signals are computed on closed bars; next-bar execution has a defined, modeled gap.
  • MT5 contract-aware sizing — risk-at-stop uses broker math, not pip shortcuts.
  • Regime-aware, per-(symbol, TF, regime) winners — strategies must prove themselves locally, not globally.
  • Calendar-anchored retrain — fixed schedule beats rolling expiry in production.
  • Quality gates stack — pre-tuning eligibility → tuning → validation gates → regime descent → live scoring margin → live winners-only → live spread overlay → broker preflight.

Every recommendation below is required to respect and strengthen these values; no recommendation intentionally relaxes the winners-only policy, candle-close semantics, or the config-first model.

Foundational correction in intent. FXPM is a broad competitive discovery system, not a curated shortlist of preferred strategies. Winners exist only at exact (symbol, TF, regime) resolution. Breadth, parameter freedom, and strategy diversity are discovery assets unless they create scientific invalidity. Efficiency improvements are acceptable only when they do not bias the tournament before discovery. Downstream governance such as holdout gates, live allocation, and post-fill order management remains compatible because it acts after winner discovery rather than contaminating it.

2b. Foundational correction in research governance

The second-pass review surfaced a deeper drift than ordinary wording: parts of the earlier write-up quietly treated pool compression, regime-balancing admission rules, and family merging as if they were obviously good. That is not the governing philosophy of FXPM. The corrections below are now adopted across this document and the companion technical-direction document.

Prior driftCorrected position
A. "From 47 strategies toward 35 high-quality strategies."FXPM is not trying to converge toward a smaller curated catalogue by default. Broad competition remains the default. Pruning is acceptable only after evidence shows exact local winners are materially unchanged.
B. "New strategies must fill a regime-coverage hole."Coverage improvement can be useful, but it is not an admission requirement. A narrow or specialized strategy is legitimate if it can win exact local tournaments.
C. "Must win on at least 3 symbols across at least 2 regimes."That rule unfairly rejects local specialists. One durable local edge can justify a strategy's existence.
D. Correlation-based redundancy pruning and family-merging tone.Structural similarity is an audit target, not a default reason to merge or evict. Similar-looking competitors may still produce different exact local winners.
E. Hard-fixing large parameter spaces such as EMARibbonADX.The problem is not breadth itself. The correct fix is fairness-preserving search governance: staged search, family-level budget controls, matched-budget experiments, or larger justified compute. Hard preconditioning requires proof it does not distort winner discovery.
F. Treating lower strategy count as an obvious good.Efficiency is subordinate to truthful discovery. Fewer, tidier strategies are not automatically better. Any narrowing must prove fairness preservation, not just elegance or maintainability.

The committed config.json is a "challenge profile" (per README.md line 54). In the drift below, positive deltas indicate the config is looser than the code default — i.e., the challenge profile allows weaker winners into the pool, precisely where the code defaults were recently tightened (PATCH_NOTES.md 2026-02-15 scoring audit). The net effect is that the safety work in the code is partially undone by operating config.

4. Scoring, validation, and backtester

Critical

Weak-train exceptional-validation rescue path is too permissive

pm_pipeline.py:2437–2462 · pm_core.py:670–672

A strategy that loses money in-sample (train PF < 1 or negative return) can still be selected as a regime winner on ~20 lucky validation trades. With code-default thresholds (val PF ≥ 1.5, val return ≥ 10%) this is already marginal; with the challenge config (1.15 / 5.0) it is a curve-fit magnet.

Proposed: (a) align config with code defaults, (b) also require val_sharpe ≥ 0.5 and val_trades ≥ 50 for the rescue path, (c) require val_win_rate > 50%. Together these stop "one lucky validation run" from overruling a losing training set.

Low

Score weights only matter in the legacy pm_weighted scoring mode

pm_core.py:708–714 (legacy weights), pm_core.py:4421–4426 (mode dispatcher), config.json:23 (active mode)

The committed config sets scoring_mode = "fx_backtester", which routes all selection scoring through calculate_fx_selection_score / calculate_fx_opt_score and bypasses the score_weights block entirely. So the original "PF→0.30, Sharpe→0.20" recommendation against the legacy block has no live effect.

Where to actually express PF emphasis in the active path: the fx_backtester scorer combines validation PF, return, DD, and trade-count stability in fx_generalization_score with a fixed gap penalty (fx_gap_penalty_lambda) and a robustness boost (fx_robustness_boost). PF emphasis already comes from how PF enters the component scores, not from a separate weight. The realistic levers are:

  • Raise fx_min_robustness_ratio (committed 0.75 → recommend 0.85). This thins train→val degradation tolerance directly; Section 3 already lists this.
  • Tighten fx_val_max_drawdown from 15.0 → 12.0 in the active path; this is the real DD penalty input.
  • If the legacy pm_weighted mode is no longer intended for live, either remove it or guard it behind a clear "legacy" comment so operators know the score_weights block in config.json is informational only.
High

Continuous drawdown penalty too soft

pm_core.py:4286–4289 (calculate_fx_selection_score)

At the fx_val_max_drawdown = 15 threshold, exp(-0.03·15) = 0.64 — a 36% penalty. A candidate at 25% DD with strong Sharpe can still win. Tighten to exp(-0.05·dd) so the penalty at 15% DD is 0.47 and at 25% is 0.29.

High

Robustness ratio default permits 20% out-of-sample degradation

pm_core.py:553

fx_min_robustness_ratio = 0.80 means a strategy can score 100 in training and 80 in validation and still be considered robust. For FX, 20% out-of-sample regression is a predictable end to live profitability. Raise to 0.90 in both code and config; expect ~10–15% pool thinning, which is the point.

High

actionable_score_margin semantics are relative, not absolute

pm_main.py:2047–2062; docstring pm_core.py:573–576

In live code, margin is applied as min_score = best_overall_score * margin (pm_main.py:2090). The SETTINGS_REFERENCE wording ("minimum score threshold") is misleading — this is a margin around the leader, not a floor. Code-default 0.92 means "candidates within 8% of leader are eligible"; config 0.90 means "within 10%". For multi-winner symbols this widens the tie-break, letting slightly weaker candidates win.

Proposed: (a) align config to 0.92; (b) rewrite the SETTINGS_REFERENCE description to "minimum fraction of the leader's selection score required to be eligible"; (c) in code, also guarantee best_overall_score > 0 before the margin is applied (so a zero-score leader doesn't silently let zero-score candidates through).

Pass

Train/val overlap is warmup-only and never scored (corrected)

pm_core.py:3229–3234 (DataSplitter docstring + contract version)

Re-read of DataSplitter shows it explicitly states "the overlap window is warmup-only and is never scored" and pins this behavior with SPLIT_CONTRACT_VERSION = "2026-03-29a". Earlier soft-look-ahead claim is unsupported by the splitter contract. Finding withdrawn.

Medium

Feature cache thrashes on multi-symbol runs

pm_core.py:2621–2693 (_FEATURE_CACHE_MAX = 6)

Current cap fits 6 symbols × 1 TF concurrent. With 10+ symbols across 6 timeframes, evictions every iteration lose tens of seconds each. Raise to 24 and add a hit-rate log line once per retrain.

Pass

Backtester correctness — sound

pm_core.py:248–422 + Numba kernel

Same-bar gap-through-stop, bid/ask spread application at entry and exit, SL-before-TP same-bar check, next-bar-open execution, and Python/Numba parity all verified. The only note is that EOD force-close exits at close ± half_spread are slightly optimistic (~0.5 pip on M5 EOD); acceptable for the timeframes you trade.

5. Strategy pool

High

Mean-reversion strategies default to 1:1 R:R

pm_strategies.py — RSIExtremes, BollingerBounce, StochasticReversal, CCIReversal, WilliamsR

Defaults to sl_mult = 2.0, tp_mult = 2.0. After commission + spread + slippage, breakeven requires ~53–55% WR even on majors. Proposed default sl_mult = 2.0, tp_mult = 3.0. Grid remains free to tune, but defaults shouldn't seed a negative-expectancy candidate.

High

Oscillator reversals lack higher-TF trend filter

pm_strategies.py — RSIExtremes:1130–1144, CCIReversal, Stochastic, Williams%R; template at RSITrendFilteredMRStrategy:2104–2109

Roughly half of "oversold" fires happen in established downtrends — a known failure mode. Apply the existing RSITrendFilteredMRStrategy pattern (EMA200 gate) to all four. Estimated +10–15% win rate on the affected candidates; keeps the entry logic otherwise unchanged.

High

EMARibbonADX search budget is imbalanced under a flat trial cap

pm_strategies.py:2052–2061

84,480 combinations is large, but large is not automatically wrong in FXPM. The real issue is fairness: with max_param_combos = 200, this family receives a materially coarser search than smaller families, so the tournament can become compute-biased rather than merit-biased. Proposed: replace hard axis-fixing with staged search (coarse template pass then focused refinement), family-level budget controls, or larger justified trial budgets for large families. Only hard-remove axes such as adx_period or use_di_confirmation if a matched-budget invariance audit shows exact local winners are preserved. Add an explicit warning in BaseStrategy.get_param_grid() when the cartesian product exceeds a threshold so operators can choose budget intentionally.

Medium

Cached helper short-circuits on hardcoded defaults may return wrong precomputed column

pm_strategies.py _get_keltner / _get_bb / _get_adx_di

Example: _get_keltner short-circuits to the precomputed KC_* columns if params equal (20, 20, 2.0). If a grid tests (20, 20, 1.5) right after (20, 20, 2.0), the helper correctly misses the short-circuit — but the precomputed columns from the prior run may still be in the DataFrame with the wrong multiplier. Make the short-circuit explicit on a per-DataFrame tag rather than column presence.

Low

Structural similarity is not automatic redundancy

pm_strategies.py (pool-wide)

4 EMA-crossover-family variants, 4 oscillator-reversal variants, and 3 BB/Keltner-band variants may look similar structurally, but similar-looking competitors can still produce different exact local winners. Treat similarity as an audit target, not an eviction rule. Merge or prune only after matched-budget backfill shows the exact local winner set is materially unchanged. The zero-trade-tail issue is separate: keep ATRPercentileBreakoutStrategy and KaufmanAMATrendStrategy visible in optimizer summaries so operators can see empty buckets without pre-judging them out of the tournament.

6. Live loop, MT5 & storage

pm_main.py same-symbol enforcement path

pm_main.py:2770–2798

Spread-spike median is timing-sensitive

pm_main.py:2739–2742 · pm_enhancement_seams.py:363–399

The rolling 50-bar median uses closed-bar spread values, then compares to the current tick spread. In pre-news compression, rolling median can be very low, so a routine widening is flagged as a spike; in post-news persistence, a high baseline median can hide actual spikes. Proposed: use a slightly longer window (e.g., 100 bars) and also compare against spec.spread_avg (whichever is higher) for the spike threshold; keep the current behavior when median is the higher anchor.

High

Lot-normalization can silently elevate risk

pm_main.py:3000–3056

When computed volume < broker min_lot, the code floors up to min_lot, which pushes risk above target. The logger reports the drift, but the trade still places. For a challenge profile this is the wrong default: if the only way to satisfy broker minimum is to exceed target risk, the correct behavior is to skip unless the post-round risk still fits inside max_risk_pct. Add an explicit check: if actual_risk_pct > target_risk_pct * (1 + tolerance) and actual_risk_pct <= max_risk_pct → log loud warning; if actual_risk_pct > max_risk_pct → block.

Medium

Margin-recovery has no cooldown before new entries

pm_main.py:1733–1812

After a PANIC close cycle lifts the account back above margin_recovery_start_level, the very next sweep accepts new trades. In flash-crash conditions this allows immediate re-entry into the same exposure that caused the panic. Proposed: add a margin_reopen_cooldown_minutes setting (default 15 min) and block all new entries for that window after any forced close cycle, regardless of current margin level.

Medium

Decision-throttle has no expiry — cross-restart hazard

pm_main.py:319–508

The throttle persists last-decision state per symbol to last_trade_log.json and is keyed by bar time. On restart after a transient skip (e.g., spread spike), if the same bar is replayed and conditions have normalized, the stale suppression sticks. Proposed: add a 24-hour max-age purge on load; alternatively, key the throttle by bar close time rather than start time so a new bar inherently invalidates.

Pass

Winners-only selection and regime-shift semantics

pm_main.py (winners loop), pm_pipeline.py (ledger)

Strict per-(symbol, TF, regime) lookup. No fallback path remains active. REGIME_LIVE.iloc[-2] correctly reads the previous closed bar. Open-position TF inference chain is complete and fail-closed.

8.3 — Optuna 80/20 blend weights are dead config (reframed)

The original framing claimed trials are selected on train-only score because optuna_use_val_in_objective=false. Re-reading the active scoring path showed this is wrong:

8.4 — Objective has no variance / stability penalty

pm_core.py:_compute_fx_generalization_score

The pipeline's generalization score penalizes drawdown and rewards PF/Sharpe/WR/ret/trades but carries no explicit term for dispersion of equity-curve returns. Two strategies with identical Sharpe can have very different bar-return variance; the second will bleed more on live spreads and slippage.

Fix: add a multiplicative penalty exp(-k · std_weekly_return) with k ≈ 2.0. Small but consistent impact on winner stability; pairs naturally with the tighter DD penalty in §6.

High

8.5 — Risk scalars disabled by default, leaving volatility targeting off

config.json (live_risk_scalars_enabled: false), pm_enhancement_seams.py:117–242

The RiskScalarStack (VolatilityTargetScalar → FractionalKellyCap → DrawdownPositionScalar) is well-constructed and correctly ordered (vol-target first, Kelly-cap second, DD-scalar last), but the master switch is off. This means:

  • A trade sized at risk_per_trade_pct on a mid-volatility USDJPY bar carries the same equity-risk as the same config on a hyper-volatile XAUUSD bar.
  • Drawdown-based de-risking is not applied — a 10 % drawdown triggers nothing at the sizing layer.
  • The committed target_annual_vol=0.10 has no effect at all.

Fix: enable the stack behind a two-stage rollout —

  1. Shadow mode: compute scalars, log them, do not apply. One full week of logs to verify the stack's realized sizes are not degenerate.
  2. Full enable with a conservative floor (min_scalar=0.5) and a cap (max_scalar=1.0) so the stack can only reduce risk, never amplify it.
High

8.6 — No daily / session loss cap at the portfolio layer

pm_main.py live loop, pm_core.py PortfolioManager

There is a per-trade cap (position.max_risk_pct), a same-symbol combined cap (max_combined_risk_pct), and a margin-recovery state machine, but no explicit daily-loss circuit-breaker. A prop-firm or challenge profile without one is effectively gambling on the drawdown-of-the-day distribution.

Fix: add position.daily_loss_halt_pct (default 3.0 %) and position.session_loss_halt_pct (default 5.0 %). When breached:

  • No new entries allowed until the next trading day (daily) or next session rollover.
  • Existing open trades are not force-closed — they continue to their SL/TP so we do not panic-realize. This matches the winners-only philosophy: the decision we revoke is "start a new trade," not "abandon a plan we already committed to."
Medium

8.7 — No regime-conditional R-multiple

pm_position.py:_compute_tp_pips

The same R-target is used whether the active regime is TREND or RANGE. TREND regime supports and rewards wider TPs (trend-continuation expectancy is asymmetric); RANGE regime is better served by tighter targets (mean-reversion mean is close). A single multiplier forces one of the two regimes into a suboptimal exit plan.

Fix: add position.regime_tp_multipliers as a dict — e.g. {"TREND":1.25, "BREAKOUT":1.15, "RANGE":0.85, "CHOP":0.75} — and apply it multiplicatively to the base TP during live entry. This composes cleanly with the existing regime-aware strategy selection; no winners-set change required.

8.8 — Pip-value fallback when MT5 order_calc_profit unavailable

pm_position.py:_estimate_risk_usd

The fallback path uses static pip-value tables. For JPY crosses and metals, the static table underestimates true pip-value when the account is funded in a non-USD currency. Not a common scenario on the current account config, but worth noting: if the MT5 primitive returns None, the system will silently under-size. An explicit WARN log (not just DEBUG) on fallback would surface this before it becomes a live PnL issue.

Pass

Lot-normalization floor, seed determinism, pm_configs.json atomic write

Broker lot step floor, min/max clipping, and tick rounding are handled correctly in _normalize_lot. Optuna's TPESampler(seed=…) is passed through with the configured seed; repeated optimizer runs with the same seed are reproducible to the trade. pm_configs.json uses atomic replace-writes; interrupted runs do not corrupt the ledger.

9. Execution-quality overlay — issues & quick wins

The spread overlay (pm_enhancement_seams.py:344–399) is conceptually correct (spike block, min-edge reject, soft penalty). Two fixes materially improve its behavior:

  1. Penalty curve saturates at 0.5. Current: penalty = max(0.5, 1 - (ratio - start)). Once spread/ATR exceeds start + 0.5, further deterioration is free. Fix: penalty = max(0.25, 1 - 0.4·(ratio - start)) — softer floor, slower decay, non-saturating at realistic ratios.
  2. Spread-spike compare base. Use max(rolling_median_50, spec.spread_avg) as anchor; prevents collapsed rolling medians from arming the blocker unfairly, and prevents inflated rolling medians from hiding real spikes.

10. Candidate new strategies (examples, not privileged admissions)

The proposals below are candidate entrants or wrappers that could be allowed to compete in the same tournament. They are not privileged because they "balance the pool"; they still must earn exact local wins under the same symbol x timeframe x regime contract as every incumbent.

  • Session-Momentum Continuation (LondonOpen, NY-Overlap): only fires during high-liquidity windows and gates on spread-quality overlay. Plausible specialist for exact local trend pockets where session structure matters.
  • Range Mean-Reversion with Volatility Filter: BB ±1.5σ fade, gated by ADX < 20 and ATR non-expanding. Plausible specialist for low-ADX local range pockets.
  • Daily-Breakout Retest: D1 breakout that closes back inside the range triggers an H4 entry in the reverse direction with a structural SL beyond the daily swing. Low-trade-count candidate for exact breakout-failure pockets.
  • Hybrid Structural SL wrapper: not a strategy, but a wrapper the base class can apply to any breakout/trend strategy — combine ATR-based SL with the nearest past swing; pick the wider of the two, clip at 1.5× ATR. Materially reduces whipsaw losses without over-tightening TP.

12. Technical direction & guiding philosophy

For a stable long-term direction, a separate TECHNICAL_DIRECTION.md document accompanies this review. The core principles encoded there (and mirrored briefly below) are intentionally framed as decision rules, not aspirations:

The FxPM 1.4 decision rules

  1. Correctness is a precondition, not a feature. Any code path that privileges speed over deterministic, causal behavior is rejected even if faster.
  2. Config is a contract between operator and strategy. If code defaults tighten, committed config must catch up before live. Drift is a defect.
  3. Winners-only means no fallback, ever. "No trade" is a first-class outcome with dedicated logging, metrics, and throttle behavior.
  4. Regime is a hard filter, not a hint. A winner in TREND does not trade in CHOP even if its signal happens to fire. Transitions are explicit.
  5. Risk is budgeted forward, not reconciled backward. Risk caps are checked at decision time against geometry-derived exposure; tags are informational, not authoritative.
  6. Backtest and live must be indistinguishable to the strategy. Any divergence (spread, slippage, gap handling, entry timing) is a debt; debts are paid off, not rationalized.
  7. Signals fire on closed bars. Anything else is look-ahead. Settle-time is a feature, not a delay.
  8. Generalization beats optimization. The robustness ratio gate, the train/val gap penalty, and the weak-train rescue thresholds all answer the same question: does this survive the next regime?
  9. Selection is a fair tournament at exact local granularity. Winners are decided per-(symbol, TF, regime), stored, and retrained on a calendar. There is no global symbol winner, and no strategy family is entitled to pre-discovery preference.
  10. Observability is execution's insurance. Every blocked entry has a reason; every margin transition has a log line; every deviation from modeled behavior is surfaced.

13. Strategic & holistic recommendations (from second-pass review)

The first twelve sections cover correctness and tactical PnL leverage. The points below are at the architectural / business level — incorporated from a holistic second-pass review and (for §13.6) re-grounded in direct measurements from fxpm_order_management_poc.ipynb. They are accepted into this document because they identify real gaps not captured by the line-level findings and they materially shape the next stage of the project.

Headline verdict from the holistic review: the project does not need a ground-up overhaul. The execution stack, persistence, MT5 safeguards, and core selection pipeline are real strengths and worth preserving. The next gains are not from collapsing the PM into one umbrella policy engine, but from improving downstream review discipline, research realism, and post-entry governance while preserving the autonomy of exact local winners.
Three-layer model (corrected). FXPM should be understood as three linked but distinct layers:
  1. Discovery tournament: find the winning strategy and parameterization for the exact local context (symbol, TF, regime).
  2. Local governance tournament: once that winner exists, test order-management variants for that same exact local context.
  3. PM backbone: provide broker calls, order flow, persistence, telemetry, and execution infrastructure so these specialized local winners can operate reliably.

That means freshness review, holdout review, and order governance should support exact local winners after discovery. They should not be framed as a universal umbrella policy that flattens local autonomy.

13.1 - Edge freshness via fixed retraining cadence and structured review (Priority 0)

Medium

Gap: the earlier framing leaned too hard toward fail-closed freshness shutdown. That is not the intended operating model. FXPM already has a defined edge-freshness mechanism: the fixed two-week retraining cycle.

Corrected recommendation: keep the two-week live window as the primary freshness anchor and improve the review discipline around it rather than overriding it mid-cycle. The practical additions should be:

  • Pre-cycle hygiene: confirm the new winner set is loaded cleanly before the live window begins.
  • In-cycle weekly review: generate a structured review report covering realized behavior, spread conditions, missed fills, holdout context, and live-vs-research drift signals.
  • End-of-cycle refresh: treat the scheduled retrain as the normal point for intervention, replacement, and re-qualification.

Mid-cycle intervention should be reserved for hard integrity failures such as corrupt artifacts, broken execution plumbing, or clearly invalid runtime state. It should not be the default response to presumed edge decay inside the active two-week window.

13.2 - Promote holdout into structured review and next-cycle context (Priority 1)

Medium
snapshot evidence: median val PF 2.06 vs median holdout PF 0.95 · 31/62 default winners have negative holdout return · 133/265 regime winners have negative holdout return

Gap: holdout metrics are informative, but the earlier framing turned them into a quasi-live gating layer. That is too intrusive for the intended operating model. Holdout should inform review discipline and next-cycle prioritization without silently shutting down local winners mid-cycle.

Corrected recommendation: extend each winner artifact with a structured review context block rather than a hidden quarantine engine:

{
  "holdout_context": {
    "pf": <float>,
    "return_pct": <float>,
    "max_dd_pct": <float>,
    "trade_count": <int>
  },
  "review_flag": "NORMAL" | "WATCH" | "REVIEW",
  "retrain_priority": <score>,
  "last_review": <timestamp>
}

This keeps the information visible and operationally useful without introducing an automatic intra-cycle deactivation layer. Holdout remains a downstream review input and next-cycle context signal, not a stealth live kill switch.

13.3 - Portfolio awareness without heavy umbrella lockout (Priority 2)

Medium

Gap: the earlier framing leaned too hard toward a central allocator with hard account budgets and cluster caps. That is philosophically misaligned if it routinely suppresses productive capital that exact local winners are ready to deploy.

Corrected recommendation: keep any portfolio layer light-touch and supportive. The order should be:

  1. Visibility first: aggregate exposure, symbol concentration, and cluster awareness in logs and dashboards.
  2. Soft sequencing second: if many local winners fire at once, allow the PM to sequence or stage submissions where it improves execution quality.
  3. Hard restrictions only for extreme states: reserve true account-level lockouts for clearly exceptional conditions rather than making them the normal umbrella policy.

The PM should default to keeping capital active and productive. A portfolio orchestration seam can still be useful, but it should act as an execution aid, not as a low-ceiling rationing engine.

13.4 - Walk-forward as audit, not a blocking promotion ladder (Priority 3)

Medium

Gap: the earlier recommendation treated an extra walk-forward promotion stage between validation and live deployment as inherently beneficial. That is not supported by the operating philosophy or by prior experience when freshness decays between tuning and live execution.

Corrected recommendation: if walk-forward remains in the framework, use it as a parallel audit tool, a post-cycle confidence study, or a research cross-check. Do not make it a blocking rung that delays movement from validation into the active live window unless it can be inserted without weakening freshness.

This preserves the value of additional robustness analysis while respecting the practical reality that late deployment can be worse than theoretically cleaner deployment.

13.5 — Cost realism upgrades (Priority 4)

Medium

Gap: backtest currently uses fixed spec.spread_avg, fixed slippage, and no swap/carry. The 1.07-pip-vs-0.95-PF gap between validation and holdout (snapshot above) is partly research-realism, not just generalization.

Recommendation:

  • Use recorded per-bar spread when available; for symbols where it isn't, model spread as a session/volatility function.
  • Replace fixed slippage with session/symbol-conditioned slippage tables.
  • Apply swap/carry in backtest PnL for any position carried across rollover.
  • Add "edge after friction" as a first-class metric and require it > 0 for promotion.

13.6 - Local OrderGovernanceEngine: governance as a second tournament after winner discovery (most material strategic addition)

High

The single largest gap in the current architecture. FXPM today enters trades with broker-side SL/TP and then leaves them mostly unmanaged. Trailing, breakeven, and time-exit logic exist in pm_position.py; MarketDrivenExitPack exists in pm_enhancement_seams.py. What is missing is a first-class live supervisor that owns an open trade through its lifecycle.

This is why the current system is still an incomplete live-trading model even though the selection skeleton is strong. Fixed exits are a useful scientific control, but they are not the full economic answer once a trade is live.

Corrected design boundary: strategy selection stays untouched. Governance begins only after a trade is filled, and it must be selected locally for the exact winner context rather than imposed as one global PM policy.

What the PM is doing here

  • Discovery tournament: choose the winning strategy and parameter set for the exact local context (symbol, TF, regime).
  • Local governance tournament: given that winner, compare post-entry governance variants for the same exact local context.
  • Technical backbone: run broker calls, persistence, telemetry, throttles, and execution plumbing so those local winners and local governance policies can operate safely.

Subsystem shape

  • OrderGovernanceEngine owned by LiveTrader
  • OrderGovernanceContext + OrderGovernanceState + OrderGovernanceDecision
  • Decision types: NOOP, MODIFY_SLTP, CLOSE_FULL, CLOSE_PARTIAL, SHADOW_ONLY
  • Reuse MarketDrivenExitPack as the policy seam, PositionManager for trail/BE/time-exit semantics, MT5 modify_position() for broker updates, and StorageManager for durable per-position state

Governance arm library (candidate policies, not universal defaults)

There is no philosophical reason two exact local contexts must share the same governance winner. Similar entry winners may still justify different governance winners.

Validation ladder (local-context competition, no contamination of selection science)

  1. Stage 1 - Offline local governance replay: replay each selected winner's bars under each governance arm and emit comparable trade metrics keyed by (winner_fingerprint, governance_variant_id).
  2. Stage 2 - Shadow live on the same local context: log hypothetical governance decisions to the ledger without modifying live orders.
  3. Stage 3 - Controlled local A/B: deterministic per-fill arm assignment, persisted; throttle identity includes the arm; control arm always stays active for measured uplift.
  4. Stage 4 - Select the governance winner for that exact local context: promote the winning governance arm locally, not across the PM globally.

Persisted governance state

Key by MT5 ticket and link it to the exact winner fingerprint. Required fields: symbol, TF, regime, strategy fingerprint, parameter fingerprint, entry/SL/TP, initial risk pips/USD, governance policy + version + arm id, MFE/MAE, bars held, last stop level, flags (breakeven_applied, partial_tp1_taken, trailing_active), and last governance action timestamp. This belongs in a dedicated durable store, not last_actionable_log.json.

Promotion criteria (must satisfy both)

  • Local economic uplift: PF lift vs control, win-quality improvement, R-multiple change, DD impact, and net return after friction for that exact local context.
  • Operational stability: MFE capture rate, giveback before exit, stop-out rate after adjustment, modify/freeze/requote failure rate, and emergency-close frequency.

13.7 - PM backbone role and optional TradeIntent seam (Priority 6, longer horizon)

Low

Gap: the PM already acts mainly as a technical backbone - broker calls, order flow, persistence, telemetry, and execution mechanics for many specialized local winners - but the contract between local winners, local governance, and the backbone is still narrow.

Recommendation: if the separation needs to become cleaner, introduce a TradeIntent typed structure carrying side, expected edge after cost, expected holding horizon, stop/target geometry, sizing bounds, and governance hints. The goal is not to centralize policy; it is to make local autonomy easier for the backbone to carry reliably.

13.8 - Recommended sequencing

  1. Keep the two-week retraining cycle as the freshness anchor and add structured weekly review inside the active window (13.1).
  2. Promote holdout into review, retrain prioritization, and downstream context metadata without turning it into a mid-cycle kill switch (13.2).
  3. Improve research realism without inserting blocking deployment delay (13.5 and the non-blocking interpretation of 13.4).
  4. Stand up OrderGovernanceEngine as a local governance tournament per exact winner context (13.6) - start with price-path-only arms (atr_trail_capped, then breakeven_1r) on the sampled contexts, and let broader contexts prove themselves locally.
  5. Add light-touch portfolio awareness only if it helps orchestration, not to impose routine umbrella lockouts on productive capital (13.3).
  6. Only then expand the orchestration contract if useful (TradeIntent, 13.7).

Why this order: the PM already has a freshness mechanism - the fixed retrain cadence. The next gains come from better review discipline, better cost realism, and local governance uplift. Heavy mid-cycle shutdown logic, blocking walk-forward ladders, or low-ceiling umbrella allocators would fight the operating philosophy instead of serving it.

Why the narrowed first cohort: the order-management notebook is the only empirical promotion evidence currently available, and its strongest signal is for two price-path arms on sampled local contexts. That is a good basis for the next local tests, not a license to impose one global governance winner across the PM.
