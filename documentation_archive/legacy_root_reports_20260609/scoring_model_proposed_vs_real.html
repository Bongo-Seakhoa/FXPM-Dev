0 · What we are replacing

The legacy scorer — a hand-weighted sum with hard-coded constants

score = 25·(risk_adj) + 20·(PF) + 15·(win_rate) + 25·(return/DD) + 15·(expectancy) … × penalty buckets

Every coefficient (25/20/15/25/15), every penalty (exp(-0.05·dd), >8 losses → ×0.97, an 8% frequency bonus…) is an arbitrary constant declaring what "good" means. The mandate: replace this with a model where the data — not magic numbers — sets the scale, and the winner is the candidate most likely to make money out-of-sample.

1 · The two models, side by side

Proposed — your vision

A path-dependent 3D quality object

  • Each candidate is a shape, not a scalar: a spider/radar of quality axes…
  • …extruded through a third dimension (validation stages / time) — an "upside-down octagonal pyramid" expanding from a convergence point to an open face.
  • Judge both: volume (total quality expansion) and shape integrity (balanced, smooth, non-concentrated, structurally stable).
  • One damaged dimension should dent its own trace — not collapse the whole object.
  • Dent detection: one big capital-preservation dent ≠ many small ripples.
  • Shape gives hard structural boundaries → early rejection of candidates that provably can't recover (the efficiency win).
  • No arbitrary constants — compare candidates against each other; the pool sets the scale.
Real — implemented analysis layer

geomean of pool-normalized axes + an axis-by-time trace gate

  • 8 axes per candidate (the radar): net_return, profit_factor, sortino, recovery, tail-CVaR, equity-R², temporal-consistency, capital-preservation. The implementation may still label this ulcer_control, but the orientation is reverse path-dependent drawdown: higher is better.
  • geomean(percentile-normalised axes) = volume × balance (AM–GM). One number that is large only if the polygon is big and regular, and collapses if any side caves.
  • selection_validation trace helpers: a pool-normalised [candidate × stage × axis] tensor → per-axis dents, retention, stability. The older topology_trace_features helper is diagnostic-only.
  • Promotion gates stay external and hard: min-trades, max-DD, holdout, PBO, stress (pass/fail). They are not all embedded inside the current optimizer objective.
  • Shape is currently used as experimental post-run selector variants, with profit-efficiency as the objective among survivors (shape_gate_*, trace_gate_*).
  • Everything is judged by multi-dimensional validation (forward return reliability · forward capital-preservation / raw DD · runtime), but those results are not yet strong enough to set a default.

2 · Figure — the spider, volume & balance

balanced shape spiky / overfit (high return, caved sides)
Illustrative balanced candidate (green) fills the octagon evenly. The dashed red candidate has big net_return/PF spokes but caved-in risk/consistency sides — high raw "volume" on two axes, but low balance. The geomean punishes that shape mathematically. This picture explains the concept; it is not a promotion claim for a live winner.

volume × balance = geomean

geomean(a₁…aₙ) = volume × balance

volume = arithmetic mean of the normalised axes (how big the polygon is).
balance = geomean ÷ volume ∈ (0,1], = 1 only when every axis is equal (a regular polygon).

This is the AM–GM identity. The geometric mean is the single number your concept asked for: it rewards a structure that is both large and undistorted, and any single damaged side drags it toward zero — "no one metric carries the whole shape."

Match: "large quality volume + balanced expansion + no distorted side" → represented by a simple operator in the analysis tools. It captures the intended pressure without needing a literal 3D mesh.

Proposed: the candidate expands stage-by-stage into a clean, balanced "upside-down octagonal pyramid." A healthy strategy widens smoothly; a damaged one bulges or caves on one face along the way.

Implemented in analysis: [stage × axis] trace, pool-normalised

Implemented analysis: each axis carries its own trace across stages. The red cell is a recovery dent at stage 2 — it damages that axis's trace (and trips trace_worst_axis) without flattening the other seven dimensions. This matches your "one damaged dimension dents itself" requirement, but it is still a post-run comparison layer until proven and promoted into the default scoring path.

4 · Figure — how a winner is actually chosen

This is the intended decision boundary, not a claim that the live optimizer has already switched defaults. Survival is pass/fail in the promotion tools. Shape acts as a post-run selector gate in the current experiment, then profit-efficiency is the objective among the shape-healthy survivors. Raw drawdown remains an audit/gate metric, but the geometry should score its reverse: capital preservation, where higher is better through evidence. The balance term here is cross-axis health inside one specialist candidate, not portfolio symbol/regime balance. The dashed validation box feeds back: a rule is only trusted if it provably picks better forward — which is exactly the box that is not yet green (see §6).
Specialist-first correction: the primary optimisation unit is symbol × timeframe × regime. The scorer should select the best live-capable specialist inside that cell. Portfolio logic stays downstream as account survival controls and diagnostics: margin safety, aggregate exposure, account drawdown, broker close-out prevention, correlation/crowding review. It should not penalize a specialist merely for being specialized unless a future experiment proves that intervention improves live outcomes without reducing edge.

Why no selection rule can be crowned yet — the persistence sign-flipped with more data

corr(selection-half return, forward-half return) across the candidate pool. At 8 symbols it looked like real persistence (selection helps); at 23 symbols it collapsed to ~zero. The early signal did not survive a larger partial sample — so any claim about which scoring rule is "best" (geomean, PF, topology) is currently unproven. This is why the model's shape can be conceptually right while its trading value remains unsettled.
Leading hypothesis, not final verdict: the biggest blocker may be upstream of the scoring shape. Partial W3 evidence repeatedly shows thin clean-holdout samples and a near-breakeven candidate pool under honest cost. A scorer cannot rescue a weak or unjudgeable pool; it can only choose among candidates that contain forward edge. The productive next questions are holdout construction (thicker window or walk-forward folds) and closing the train→OOS gap — then the geometry can be tested fairly.

Verdict on your question — “is the conceptual image the same?”  Mostly yes for the analysis/selector design: volume × balance + axis-by-time trace + dent-aware gating represent the image you described. No for a stronger claim: it is not yet the production-default scorer, and it is not yet proven to improve OOS profitability. Full proof awaits the 62-symbol completion review with stress gates.
